## Draft proposal

Who gets to decide what a listening trace means? Storywalker asks how an autobiographical music system can reuse platform data while giving neurodivergent listeners control over pace, disclosure and interpretation.

Listening Again is a bounded browser study: one fictional encounter, a separate playlist trace and a present voice. A deliberately reassuring proposal can be accepted, rewritten, refused or left unresolved. Each choice changes a spatial connection and its optional synthetic sound while retaining source evidence. Dated readings can disagree; silence and withheld meaning remain possible. A playlist entry is not a listening event, and a studio recording cannot stand for a live cover.

The study brings platform-mediated listening into dialogue with living archives and the politics of comfortable speech. Liz Pelly's Mood Machine is a reading in progress. Text equivalents, keyboard controls, low-volume playback and pauses make access part of the method. I propose to present the working study and questions from its development, clearly separating software behaviour from any later Author observations. Participatory XR remains a future horizon; no therapeutic or community outcomes are claimed.

For Process…ing, the rejectable edit asks whether a system can leave time and authority with the person whose memory is at stake.
